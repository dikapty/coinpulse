# US Bill H.R. 8957 Proposes 20-Year Lock-Up on Federal Strategic Bitcoin Holdings

Proposed United States legislation introduced as bill H.R. 8957 seeks to establish strict federal oversight and a long-term lock-up period on the U.S. government's strategic Bitcoin reserves. Under the explicit provisions outlined in the legislative text, the federal government would be legally barred from selling its strategic Bitcoin holdings for a duration of 20 years.

As reported by [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/us-government-could-be-barred-from-selling-its-strategic-bitcoin-holdings-for-20-years/), the statutory framework proposed in H.R. 8957 pairs this mandatory two-decade sales restriction with comprehensive reporting and verification protocols. The dual mechanism is designed to maintain strict transparency and legal oversight over sovereign digital asset holdings held by federal entities.

## Mandatory Auditing and Public Cryptographic Attestation

Beyond establishing the 20-year sales prohibition, H.R. 8957 outlines specific technical and legal oversight requirements aimed at ensuring continuous balance verification. The legislation mandates an annual reserve report alongside public cryptographic attestation to confirm federal reserve balances.

Under this mandated auditing framework, independent verification processes must be executed to certify that reported Bitcoin balances remain fully secured within designated government-controlled wallet addresses. These procedures are intended to confirm that the assets undergo no unauthorized transfers, sales, or financial encumbrances.

By integrating public cryptographic attestation, the bill allows outside observers to verify blockchain balance proofs directly. This mechanism combines traditional legislative auditing practices with native public blockchain verification techniques to create an auditable legal standard for federal digital asset custody.

## Shifting Away From Active Federal Asset Liquidations

Historically, digital assets acquired by the United States federal government have originated primarily through law enforcement operations, regulatory forfeitures, and criminal asset seizures. Under standard operating procedures, agencies such as the U.S. Marshals Service have routinely liquidated seized cryptocurrency balances through periodic public auctions or open-market sales.

If enacted into law, H.R. 8957 would fundamentally transform this historical liquidation model. By formalizing a strategic reserve framework and imposing a statutory 20-year moratorium on liquidations, the bill effectively isolates federal Bitcoin reserves from regular liquidation pipelines.

Establishing clear custody frameworks and independent verification requirements directly targets long-standing policy uncertainties regarding how national treasuries and federal law enforcement agencies manage accumulated digital asset stockpiles.
