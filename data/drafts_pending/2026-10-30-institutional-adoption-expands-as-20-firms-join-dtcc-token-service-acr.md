# Institutional Adoption Expands as 20 Firms Join DTCC Token Service Across 34 Eligible Securities

Financial market infrastructure continues its gradual alignment with distributed ledger technology as 20 financial institutions have officially joined the Depository Trust & Clearing Corporation (DTCC) token service. According to coverage from [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/20-institutions-join-dtcc-token-service-but-only-34-securities-qualify/), the growing institutional participation marks an important milestone in moving capital market workflows onto blockchain networks. However, rigid eligibility criteria currently limit the total number of approved assets, restricting active participation to 34 qualifying securities.

This measured expansion reflects a deliberate strategy by clearing institutions and market participants to explore digital asset technology while managing systemic risk. Rather than converting entire market catalogs at once, the current framework establishes a controlled environment where institutional participants can test tokenized workflows across a strictly vetted pool of eligible financial instruments.

## Around-the-Clock Wallet Transfers and Business-Hour Constraints

The operational structure of the DTCC token service relies on a distinct separation between secondary token movements and primary asset conversions. According to [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/20-institutions-join-dtcc-token-service-but-only-34-securities-qualify/), securities that have already undergone the tokenization process can be transferred continuously between registered institutional wallets. These pre-tokenized assets are eligible for transfer throughout the entire weekend, offering continuous, 24/7 wallet-to-wallet movement for participating market entities.

In contrast, primary onboarding and conversion processes remain tethered to traditional market schedules. While on-chain transfers of existing tokens occur without interruption over weekends, formal conversion orders—the process of converting traditional real-world financial assets into tokenized formats, or redeeming tokens back into conventional assets—must be submitted during standard business hours. Participating institutions are required to plan conversion workflows around traditional operating schedules even as they utilize 24/7 transferability for active tokens.

## Balancing Market Innovation with Operational Controls

This operational framework illustrates how traditional financial infrastructure providers are integrating blockchain technology without disrupting existing risk management protocols. By enabling weekend transfers between approved digital wallets, the platform offers institutions enhanced operational flexibility and faster settlement capability during hours when traditional clearing systems are typically offline.

At the same time, restricting token conversions to standard business hours ensures that primary creation and redemption mechanisms remain aligned with legacy banking systems, liquidity buffers, and regulatory oversight structures. This division allows clearing houses and institutional participants to evaluate liquidity management and counterparty risks in real-time without exposing primary reserve mechanisms to off-hours volatility.

## Strict Eligibility Criteria and Industry Context

The requirement that securities satisfy explicit baseline conditions explains why only 34 securities currently qualify for inclusion under the platform's tokenization framework. These stringent standards highlight the thorough risk modeling, legal review, and technical auditing required before conventional equities or debt instruments are authorized for digital token workflows.

The deployment underscores a broader trend across traditional finance, where legacy infrastructure operators are deploying permissioned or public ledger solutions in phases. As the 20 participating institutions gain operational experience and monitor transaction throughput across the 34 qualifying securities, the standards developed during this initial implementation may serve as a reference point for future digital asset integrations across global capital markets.
