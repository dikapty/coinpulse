# CFTC Staff Issue Guidance Allowing Bitcoin and Ether to Count Toward Derivatives Firms' Operating Expenses

Staff at the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) have issued a new staff advisory allowing regulated derivatives firms to count major cryptocurrencies—specifically Bitcoin (BTC) and Ether (ETH)—toward their operating expenses. The regulatory guidance provides clear operational instructions for institutional financial entities attempting to integrate digital assets into their corporate standard accounting structures.

Under the newly released advisory, qualifying derivatives firms subject to CFTC oversight may utilize held digital assets to meet specific operational expense requirements. By explicitly identifying Bitcoin and Ether for this financial purpose, the CFTC staff guidance provides established market participants with standardized compliance procedures for handling digital asset balance sheets.

This decision arrives amid ongoing regulatory discussions regarding the formal treatment of digital assets under federal commodities laws. The explicit inclusion of Bitcoin and Ether in operational accounting calculations grants regulated entities expanded flexibility when managing operational reserves and daily treasury obligations.

## Broadening Institutional Use Cases for Digital Assets

The CFTC staff advisory marks a practical step forward in how regulatory agency staff view liquid digital assets held by institutional market participants. Historically, regulated derivatives entities—including registered futures commission merchants and trading venues—have adhered to strict accounting guidelines regarding which specific liquid assets qualify to fulfill financial obligations and operational accounting thresholds.

By confirming that Bitcoin and Ether can count toward operating expenses, the advisory provides regulatory clarity for entities seeking to hold crypto assets directly on their corporate balance sheets. For market participants operating in digital asset derivatives markets, the ability to apply these holdings toward operational requirements removes structural friction between traditional fiat operational accounting and corporate digital asset treasuries.

It is important to note that this advisory reflects staff guidance for eligible market participants and does not constitute financial advice for general retail investors.

## Regulatory Context and Market Implications

The decision by CFTC staff to single out Bitcoin and Ether reinforces the specific status those two digital assets hold within the federal financial regulatory framework. Regulators have routinely treated Bitcoin and Ether differently from other digital tokens, classifying them under CFTC oversight as commodities.

Allowing derivatives firms to apply these specific digital assets to operational costs reflects their liquidity profile and established role across institutional markets. As derivatives venues and cleared market participants expand their operational touchpoints with digital assets, staff advisories help establish uniform compliance protocols for corporate auditors, financial officers, and compliance executives.

This operational flexibility may encourage further participation from traditional derivatives entities that previously hesitated to hold digital assets due to regulatory ambiguity surrounding capital and operational expense treatment.

## Integration with Broader Market Developments

The CFTC staff advisory arrives during a broader period of active institutional participation and regulatory focus across the digital asset market. Institutional firms continue to integrate Bitcoin and Ether into standard treasury balance sheets, while regulators and courts continue establishing operational rules surrounding digital asset transactions.

Within this broader industry framework, precise regulatory clarity regarding operating expenses provides derivatives market entities with a defined compliance mechanism to structure their operational crypto holdings alongside standard financial obligations.

As digital asset regulatory structures evolve, staff guidance from agencies like the CFTC remains key to how institutional entities maintain corporate balance sheets, manage liquidity risks, and incorporate digital assets into established financial services operations.
