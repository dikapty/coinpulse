# JPMorgan Research Model Outlines $385,000 Bitcoin Bull Case Scenario Over 10-to-15-Year Horizon

Wall Street investment bank JPMorgan has modeled a long-term bull scenario that projects Bitcoin reaching a price of $385,000 over a 10-to-15-year horizon. The figure surfaced in a report page shared publicly on social media platform X by Matt Hougan, Chief Investment Officer of Bitwise, as reported by [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/jpmorgan-models-bitcoin-reaching-385000-in-10-year-bull-case-despite-ceo-jamie-dimons-criticism/). 

The long-term valuation scenario stands in contrast to the public stance of JPMorgan Chairman and CEO Jamie Dimon, who has consistently expressed vocal skepticism regarding the primary cryptocurrency. Despite outlining a model that projects significant upside for Bitcoin over the next decade and beyond, the financial institution continues to refrain from recommending a formal allocation to the asset in its standard investment portfolios.

## Institutional Research Models vs. Executive Rhetoric

The emergence of the $385,000 target highlights a distinct division between internal analytical forecasting at major financial institutions and public commentary from senior leadership. While Dimon has repeatedly voiced criticism regarding Bitcoin and the broader digital asset ecosystem, quantitative researchers and analysts within JPMorgan continue to evaluate the market trajectory of digital assets over multi-decade timeframes.

According to details revealed in the report snapshot shared by Hougan, the $385,000 projection represents a specific long-term bull case scenario. Quantitative financial models of this nature routinely calculate potential valuations by examining variable growth drivers, including long-term adoption rates, fixed asset supply constraints, macroeconomic trends, and potential market share capture relative to traditional store-of-value assets such as gold. 

However, as [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/jpmorgan-models-bitcoin-reaching-385000-in-10-year-bull-case-despite-ceo-jamie-dimons-criticism/) emphasizes, the inclusion of an asset within theoretical analytical frameworks does not mean the firm actively endorses or recommends the asset class to its wealth management clients or corporate treasury clients.

## Portfolio Advice Remains Precautionary

Despite mapping out a theoretical six-figure valuation target, JPMorgan has maintained a conservative operational posture. The bank has not translated these analytical projections into formal client allocation recommendations. The institution continues to stop short of advising investors to allocate capital directly into Bitcoin within its standard wealth management frameworks.

This gap between long-term research modeling and formal investment advice illustrates how risk management protocols operate inside major Wall Street firms. Institutional investment groups utilize long-term stress testing, scenario planning, and quantitative modeling to evaluate extreme downside and upside possibilities. Generating a theoretical projection allows a bank to prepare for structural shifts in global capital markets without obligating the firm to issue active buying mandates or take direct risk positions on behalf of clients.

## Long-Term Modeling in Institutional Finance

The report page shared by Hougan provides broader insight into how global investment banks examine alternative assets outside of short-term trading windows. While retail market analysis frequently focuses on immediate price action, technological updates, or sentiment shifts, institutional research models evaluate potential multi-decade asset evolution.

Market participants often examine analytical outputs from institutional banking research as indicators of how traditional finance approaches emerging asset classes. However, institutional research models are speculative, scenario-based forecasting tools that depend heavily on underlying long-term assumptions. They do not guarantee future performance, nor do they constitute financial advice or investment recommendations. 

The divergence between JPMorgan’s quantitative projections and its cautious client advisory stance reflects the ongoing structural challenge traditional banking institutions face when assessing decentralized digital assets against legacy financial structures.
