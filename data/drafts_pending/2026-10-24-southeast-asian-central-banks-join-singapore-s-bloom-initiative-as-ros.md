# Southeast Asian Central Banks Join Singapore's BLOOM Initiative as Roster Expands to 24 Firms

The regional momentum behind digital currency initiatives across Southeast Asia took a significant step forward on October 23, 2026. According to reporting by [CryptoPotato](https://cryptopotato.com/malaysia-and-thailand-central-banks-join-singapores-bloom-as-roster-hits-24-firms/), the central banks of Malaysia and Thailand have officially joined Singapore's BLOOM initiative, expanding the project's network to a total of 24 participating organizations.

This expansion highlights growing interest among regulatory bodies and major financial institutions in regional digital asset framework collaboration. The inclusion of new central bank authorities and commercial banking partners signals an ongoing commitment to exploring tokenized finance and stablecoin infrastructure across national borders.

## Broadening Central Bank Participation in Regional Infrastructure

The integration of Bank Negara Malaysia and the Bank of Thailand into Singapore's BLOOM project brings major monetary authorities directly into the collaborative framework. The arrival of these central banks reflects a coordinated drive toward establishing cross-border standards and exploring interconnected digital asset systems within the region.

The BLOOM roster added nine new participants in its latest expansion phase, taking the total tally of member organizations from 15 to 24. By bringing diverse monetary policy regulators and private financial institutions together under a single framework, the initiative provides a structured environment to evaluate shared technology protocols and settle regulatory considerations across jurisdictions.

## Commercial Banking Engagement Drives Domestic Pilots

The expansion of the BLOOM project is deeply tied to ongoing commercial banking pilots across the individual member states. Among the nine newly added participants is Maybank, one of Malaysia's primary banking institutions. Maybank operates one of Bank Negara Malaysia's three ringgit stablecoin and tokenized deposit pilots, positioning the institution at the intersection of domestic sandbox testing and regional coordination.

Similarly, Thailand's involvement reflects established private-sector groundwork. Commercial lenders like Kasikornbank were already participating within the BLOOM roster before the Bank of Thailand officially joined the project as a regulatory authority. This progression—where commercial entities initiate operational testing alongside central banks—demonstrates how private sector pilots and regulatory oversight are aligning to build supervisory frameworks.

## Focus on Tokenized Deposits and Stablecoins

The inclusion of stablecoin and tokenized deposit initiatives inside institutional sandboxes like BLOOM emphasizes the financial industry's focus on modernizing payment settlement systems. Tokenized deposits represent digital claims on commercial bank reserves, whereas stablecoins offer digital payment vehicles tied to fiat currencies like the Malaysian ringgit or Singapore dollar.

Participating in cross-border testing platforms allows commercial banks and central monetary authorities to analyze key operational challenges, including inter-ledger interoperability, settlement mechanics, and compliance oversight. As cross-border transactions continue to evolve, building standards for tokenized value transfer is seen as a step toward evaluating efficiency and settlement times relative to traditional banking networks.

## Operational Implications and Regional Frameworks

For regional payment systems, the growth of the BLOOM initiative to 24 participants marks a key milestone in multi-jurisdictional financial testing. Integrating regulatory perspectives across Singapore, Malaysia, and Thailand helps address fragmented digital currency policies and establishes parameters for private institutions operating across these markets.

However, market participants and institutions must address ongoing technological, governance, and regulatory requirements before these pilot mechanisms can transition into full-scale deployments.
