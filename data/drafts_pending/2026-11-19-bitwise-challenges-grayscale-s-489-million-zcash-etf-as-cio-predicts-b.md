# Bitwise Challenges Grayscale’s $489 Million Zcash ETF as CIO Predicts Bitcoin-to-ZEC Rotation

Institutional competition around Zcash has intensified following a new product proposal from asset manager Bitwise. The firm is directly challenging Grayscale’s $489 million Zcash exchange-traded fund (ETF) by proposing an alternative fund structure, introducing key differences in how redemptions are handled for the privacy-focused cryptocurrency.

The development comes alongside commentary from Bitwise's Chief Investment Officer (CIO), who predicts a potential capital rotation from Bitcoin (BTC) into Zcash (ZEC). The simultaneous ETF filing and market outlook highlight shifting institutional interest toward privacy-centric digital assets and alternative fund mechanisms.

## In-Kind vs. Cash Redemptions: The ETF Structure Battle

According to reporting by [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/bitwise-challenges-grayscales-489-million-zcash-etf-as-cio-predicts-bitcoin-to-zec-rotation/), the primary point of differentiation between the two fund managers centers on their underlying redemption mechanics. Bitwise’s proposal would allow eligible firms to take physical delivery of ZEC tokens upon redemption. 

Conversely, Grayscale’s recent quarterly filing indicates that its $489 million Zcash ETF requires cash redemptions. Cash-redeemed ETFs compel fund sponsors to liquidate underlying assets to return cash to authorized participants, whereas in-kind redemptions permit authorized participants to exchange fund shares directly for the underlying cryptocurrency.

The structural divergence between in-kind and cash creations and redemptions has long been a central topic in cryptocurrency ETF structuring. In-kind redemption models are generally viewed by market participants as more tax-efficient and direct, while cash models require extra steps involving liquidity providers and third-party execution to sell digital assets for cash before clearing redemptions.

## Executive Outlook and Market Projections

Alongside the structural contest between fund managers, Bitwise's CIO has put forward a market thesis predicting a rotation of capital from Bitcoin into Zcash. This perspective suggests that market participants could reallocate a portion of their Bitcoin holdings toward ZEC as institutional infrastructure around privacy assets matures.

Adding to the broader market discourse around ZEC, industry figure Arthur Hayes recently shared upside targets for the privacy token. As covered by [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/arthur-hayes-targets-1800-for-zcash-nearly-double-its-nov-18-close/), Hayes issued projections for several cryptocurrencies, including ETH, ZEC, NEAR, and VVV. 

His targets represented upside moves ranging from 57.8% to 97.3% above their closing prices recorded on Coinbase on November 18, 2026. For Zcash specifically, Hayes targeted a price of $1,800, which stood at nearly double its November 18 close. Notably, these public targets came just weeks after Hayes had exited trades across the assets.

## Structural Implications for Digital Asset Funds

The competing product designs from Bitwise and Grayscale highlight ongoing regulatory and operational considerations for cryptocurrency ETFs. Managing funds tied to privacy-preserving blockchains introduces unique custodial and compliance workflows compared to standard transparent ledgers.

For institutional investors, the availability of an in-kind redemption mechanism can significantly alter portfolio management strategies. In-kind delivery allows market makers to arbitrage discounts or premiums on fund shares without triggering forced asset sales in the open market, potentially offering tighter tracking of the underlying spot price of ZEC.

However, regulatory oversight of privacy-focused digital assets remains rigorous. How regulators treat in-kind transfers for tokens utilizing zero-knowledge cryptography will be a critical factor in whether Bitwise's proposed ETF structure secures approval in its current form.

## Market Context and Institutional Dynamics

The struggle between asset managers for Zcash market share occurs within a changing landscape for institutional crypto products. Grayscale's incumbent position with its $489 million vehicle gives it established scale, but cash-only redemption mandates can impose operational friction for specialized arbitrage traders.

Bitwise's attempt to capture market share through an in-kind redemption model mirrors historical patterns in financial markets, where secondary entrants seek to displace incumbent funds by offering alternative structures or enhanced operational flexibility.

As the institutional crypto market evolves, product issuers are increasingly forced to compete not just on brand recognition, but on microstructural mechanics. Whether a capital rotation from Bitcoin into privacy assets such as Zcash materializes as predicted by Bitwise's leadership will depend heavily on market conditions, regulatory developments, and broader investor demand for privacy-focused blockchain networks.
