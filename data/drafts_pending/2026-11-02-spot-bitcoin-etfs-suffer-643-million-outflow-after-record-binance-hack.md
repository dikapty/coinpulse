# Spot Bitcoin ETFs Suffer $643 Million Outflow After Record Binance Hack and THORChain Freeze

Institutional and retail digital asset markets experienced sharp turbulence heading into November 2026 following a security breach at Binance that triggered capital flight across centralized and decentralized ecosystems. United States spot bitcoin exchange-traded funds (ETFs) recorded their largest single-day redemption in four months, shedding $643 million on Friday, October 30, 2026. 

The substantial ETF retrenchment followed Thursday's cyberattack against Binance, described as the world's biggest-ever cryptocurrency hack. According to reporting by [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/markets/2026-11-01-spot-bitcoin-etfs-largest-four-months-following-binance-hack-432270), the $643 million daily outflow stands as the largest single-day loss for US spot bitcoin ETFs since June 25, 2026. The institutional pullout reflects heightened market anxiety as the broader industry scrambles to contain the fallout from the exchange exploit.

## Capital Flees Binance Upon Withdrawal Reopening

In the immediate wake of the security incident, Binance temporarily suspended asset movements to secure its infrastructure. Upon restoring service, the platform faced a massive wave of capital outflows as users moved to secure their funds. As detailed by [CoinTelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/magazine/36b-flees-binance-on-reopen-thorchain-co-founder-opposes-coin-freeze-hodlers-digest?utm_source=rss_feed&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=rss_partner_inbound), approximately $3.6 billion fled the exchange shortly after withdrawals were reopened.

Despite the operational pressure on the platform, Binance founder Changpeng "CZ" Zhao publicly acknowledged law enforcement support during the crisis, specifically thanking the United States Secret Service for their assistance in the aftermath of the security breach. However, the operational restoration did little to stem broader systemic panic, as market participants rapidly de-risked their positions across both centralized trading venues and traditional vehicle access points like spot ETFs.

## THORChain Halts Swaps Amid Technical Bug and Law Enforcement Demands

As the perpetrators of the Binance exploit sought to move the stolen funds, attention shifted to decentralized cross-chain liquidity protocols. According to reporting from [CoinTelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/thorchain-halts-swaps-after-block-reorg-bug-slowing-binance-hackers?utm_source=rss_feed&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=rss_partner_inbound), THORChain node operators stepped in to pause cross-chain swaps involving assets across 10 major blockchain networks, including Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana, and Tron. The protocol's operational halt occurred after a block reorganization bug surfaced, which served to slow down the hackers' movement of assets across disparate blockchains.

The intervention highlighted growing tension between decentralized protocols and federal law enforcement agencies. While node operators halted swap functionality due to the bug, friction emerged over regulatory compliance. A co-founder of THORChain publicly opposed requests from the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) demanding that the protocol halt operations or execute coin freezes on stolen assets. The co-founder argued that the decentralized protocol should not permanently stop its operations or implement targeted coin freezes to comply with FBI demands, underscoring fundamental debates regarding governance over decentralized liquidity networks.

## Global Central Banks Investigate Systemic Contagion Risks

The fallout from the Binance breach and subsequent market disruption has prompted regulatory scrutiny worldwide regarding crypto-related financial risk. The central bank of South Korea responded to the heightened volatility by launching an investigation into domestic financial exposure. As reported by [CoinTelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/bank-of-korea-probes-financial-firms-exposure-to-crypto-risks?utm_source=rss_feed&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=rss_partner_inbound), the Bank of Korea initiated a survey targeting 31 financial institutions to evaluate their exposure to digital asset risks.

The Bank of Korea's probe focuses on assessing how potential market shocks—such as a major cryptocurrency exchange shutdown, a prolonged market downturn, or catastrophic losses at an external digital asset firm—could impact the stability and operations of traditional financial institutions. The regulatory inquiry demonstrates how severe security breaches and market dislocations prompt monetary authorities to systematically evaluate direct and indirect contagion channels.
