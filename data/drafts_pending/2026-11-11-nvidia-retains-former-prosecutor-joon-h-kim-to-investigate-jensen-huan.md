# Nvidia Retains Former Prosecutor Joon H. Kim to Investigate Jensen Huang Espionage Allegations

Nvidia has hired former prosecutor Joon H. Kim to lead an independent investigation into allegations involving Chief Executive Officer Jensen Huang and a potential Chinese spy operation. As reported by [MarketWatch](https://www.marketwatch.com/story/meet-the-former-prosecutor-nvidia-hired-to-investigate-the-jensen-huang-spy-scandal-e8078f67?mod=mw_rss_topstories) on November 10, 2026, Kim will head the internal probe to examine the circumstances surrounding the reported espionage activity targeting the company's chief executive.

The appointment brings a prominent corporate investigator into an enterprise at the center of the global technology sector. The selection of outside counsel underlines the legal and governance scrutiny surrounding allegations of foreign intelligence operations directed at executive leadership within major American technology firms.

## Background on Corporate Lead Investigator Joon H. Kim

Joon H. Kim brings extensive public sector experience to the Nvidia investigation. According to reporting by [MarketWatch](https://www.marketwatch.com/story/meet-the-former-prosecutor-nvidia-hired-to-investigate-the-jensen-huang-spy-scandal-e8078f67?mod=mw_rss_topstories), Kim spent more than a decade as a federal prosecutor in the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Southern District of New York (SDNY). During his tenure at SDNY, he served in several top leadership positions, including Deputy U.S. Attorney and Acting U.S. Attorney.

Throughout his career as a prosecutor, Kim handled complex criminal investigations involving public corruption, financial fraud, cybercrime, and national security matters. He later joined the law firm Cleary Gottlieb Steen & Hamilton as a partner, where he chaired the firm's white-collar defense and investigations practice. Last month, Kim moved his practice to the law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz, where he continues to focus on high-stakes corporate investigations, regulatory defense, and crisis management.

## High-Profile Inquiries and Executive Oversight Experience

Kim is widely recognized for leading major independent probes involving high-profile institutions and public figures. As detailed by [MarketWatch](https://www.marketwatch.com/story/meet-the-former-prosecutor-nvidia-hired-to-investigate-the-jensen-huang-spy-scandal-e8078f67?mod=mw_rss_topstories), he led the 2021 independent investigation into sexual harassment allegations against former New York Governor Andrew Cuomo. The findings of that investigation ultimately contributed to the governor's resignation.

In addition to state-level inquiries, Kim has overseen significant investigations across the corporate and sports landscapes. His past work includes leading independent reviews for the board of trustees of Michigan State University regarding institutional responses to misconduct, as well as conducting an independent investigation for the National Women's Soccer League (NWSL) into systemic abuse and executive misconduct allegations within the league.

## Scope of the Nvidia Investigation and Industry Context

The inquiry at Nvidia centers on potential foreign intelligence gathering directed at CEO Jensen Huang. While specific operational details of the alleged espionage efforts remain closely held, Nvidia's retention of an established external legal figure indicates that the board and executive team are conducting a formal review of the matter.

Semiconductor design and manufacturing firms occupy a central role in advanced computing infrastructure and global supply chains. As a result, corporate governance standards and security protocols surrounding top leadership teams have become critical operational priorities for technology hardware companies operating internationally.

## Corporate Governance and Next Steps for Stakeholders

By tasking Kim and his team at Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz with the inquiry, Nvidia is establishing a formal factual record regarding the alleged foreign spying activity. Independent corporate reviews typically evaluate the validity of underlying claims, examine internal security protocols, and provide findings to corporate boards to address compliance and risk exposure.
