# Financial Institution Projects Silver Price to Average $70 Per Ounce in 2027

A long-term precious metals forecast released on November 16, 2026, projects that silver could reach an annual average price of $70 per ounce throughout 2027. According to market analysis reported by [Finbold](https://finbold.com/banking-giant-sets-silver-price-for-2027/), a major banking institution has outlined an optimistic multi-year outlook for the metal, anticipating that valuation spikes during specific quarters could push prices toward higher historical levels.

The institution's projections indicate that silver's upward momentum is expected to concentrate around the middle of 2027. Specifically, the forecast models show price levels potentially reaching $75 per ounce during both the second quarter (Q2) and third quarter (Q3) of 2027 before settling into the broader $70 annual average.

## Mid-Year Price Targets for 2027

The long-range trajectory provided in the report establishes a detailed timeline for how silver is expected to perform across different phases of 2027. While the baseline expectation sets the overall annual average at $70 per ounce, the peak strength is projected for the mid-year quarters.

Reaching $75 per ounce in Q2 and Q3 of 2027 suggests institutional expectations of tightening market conditions during that period. Long-term bank forecasts carry weight across global markets, providing reference points for institutional investors, industrial consumers, and commodity desks evaluating asset allocations over multi-year horizons.

A projected annual average of $70 per ounce marks a significant structural price target for silver. Unlike gold, which functions primarily as a monetary and reserve asset, silver maintains a dual identity in global markets as both a financial asset and an essential industrial commodity.

## Drivers of Institutional Commodity Outlooks

Long-term institutional projections for precious metals typically incorporate broader macroeconomic trends alongside physical supply and demand metrics. When banking institutions publish targets reaching up to $75 per ounce, these models generally reflect assumptions regarding physical market balances, industrial consumption rates, and global monetary environments. Corporate procurement teams and institutional traders monitor such long-term target revisions to adjust hedging strategies and long-range supply contracts.

Extended price forecasts extending into 2027 allow market participants to compare projected commodity returns against other asset classes, including fixed income, equities, and alternative investments.
