# Canada Proposes Opening Stablecoin Access to Foreign Institutions while Excluding Deposit Guarantees

In a legislative shift for digital asset regulation, Canada's Budget 2026 has introduced measures regarding how financial entities operate within the country's fiat-backed digital currency ecosystem. According to reporting by [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/canada-opens-bank-stablecoin-access-but-strips-deposit-guarantees/), the newly proposed framework expands access to stablecoin activities while establishing explicit limits on state-backed financial protections.

The legislative proposal specifically targets how non-traditional banking structures interact with Canada's legal architecture for digital assets. By clarifying the regulatory boundaries under the nation's Stablecoin Act, federal lawmakers are seeking to balance institutional market participation with systemic risk mitigation.

## Expanding Scope Under the Stablecoin Act

The central pillar of the Budget 2026 proposal involves bringing bank subsidiaries and foreign financial institutions operating outside traditional Canadian prudential supervision under the scope of the Stablecoin Act, as reported by [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/canada-opens-bank-stablecoin-access-but-strips-deposit-guarantees/). This move expands the categories of financial entities eligible to engage with stablecoins in the Canadian market.

Under the proposed rules, foreign financial entities and subsidiary branches that do not fall under standard domestic prudential oversight will be permitted to participate in stablecoin operations within the national framework. This adjustment integrates previously uncaptured foreign and secondary banking entities into a defined federal supervisory structure.

## The Removal of Deposit Guarantees

While Budget 2026 broadens access for these entities under the Stablecoin Act, it introduces a critical constraint regarding consumer and institutional protections. As detailed by [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/canada-opens-bank-stablecoin-access-but-strips-deposit-guarantees/), the proposal explicitly strips deposit guarantees for these participating entities.

By excluding bank subsidiaries and foreign financial institutions operating outside Canadian prudential supervision from standard deposit insurance, the federal government establishes a clear separation between traditional bank deposits and stablecoin holdings. Entities entering the stablecoin ecosystem under this expanded mandate will operate without the safety net of official deposit insurance protections typically afforded to conventional bank accounts.

## Industry Context and Institutional Impact

The decision to permit expanded stablecoin access while denying deposit guarantees reflects an evolving approach to managing digital asset integration within national financial systems. Regulators face the ongoing challenge of enabling participation from international institutions while shielding public financial safety nets from crypto asset risks.

For foreign financial institutions and bank subsidiaries, the proposal offers a legal pathway to issue or service stablecoins in Canada, provided they comply with the Stablecoin Act. However, the absence of deposit guarantees shifts the risk profile directly onto market participants and token holders. Institutions operating under this structure will need to manage liquidity and reserve management transparently to maintain market confidence without federal backing.
