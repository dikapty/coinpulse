# SEC Filings Reveal Valuation Gap for Ripple Shares Across Investment Funds

Regulatory disclosures submitted to the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) have highlighted a substantial valuation discrepancy for private equity shares in Ripple Labs. According to recent filings, two separate investment vehicles hold private equity positions in the blockchain payment company at drastically different cost bases, demonstrating a valuation gap reaching roughly 70% between their respective entry points.

The regulatory documentation reveals that fund manager C1 purchased private shares in Ripple at approximately $154 per share in March. In contrast, the Private Shares Fund holds a legacy position in the firm acquired in 2018 for roughly $87 per share. The stark contrast underscores the complex landscape of evaluating private crypto-adjacent entities across different market cycles.

## Dissecting the Share Price Gap Across Investment Vehicles

According to the [SEC filings reported by CryptoPotato](https://cryptopotato.com/ripple-valuation-gap-hits-70-across-two-funds-sec-filing-shows/), the pricing variance reflects entries made during entirely different macroeconomic and regulatory environments for the digital asset industry. C1's entry in March of this year at approximately $154 per share represents a significant premium over the historical cost basis maintained by the Private Shares Fund.

The Private Shares Fund originally secured its stake in Ripple in 2018 at roughly $87 per share. That entry occurred during an earlier era of market expansion, prior to years of regulatory scrutiny and changing market dynamics. The roughly 70% valuation gap between these two figures highlights how private market transactions can diverge significantly based on timing, liquidity terms, and institutional valuation metrics.

## Understanding Valuation Dynamics in Private Equity Markets

In public financial markets, asset prices clear continuously on open exchanges, ensuring immediate price discovery. However, private equity valuations for technology and blockchain ventures depend heavily on isolated funding rounds, secondary market transactions, and periodic fund appraisals. The variance between $87 and $154 per share illustrates the challenge institutional investors face when pricing illiquid stakes in primary industry players.

For institutional allocators, large valuation spreads in SEC filings draw attention to how different funds mark their private portfolio assets. While one fund records an entry point reflecting recent private terms from March, another retains a legacy cost basis established years prior in 2018. This disparity can impact net asset value calculations, reporting metrics, and performance comparisons across funds holding exposure to the same underlying enterprise.

## Factors Influencing Private Entity Valuations

Evaluating non-public companies in the digital asset space involves assessing several variables that do not apply to listed equities. Private market participants must factor in transaction timing, illiquidity discounts, specific contractual rights attached to share classes, and broader venture capital sentiment. Because private shares do not trade continuously on a transparent public order book, individual fund holdings frequently reflect historical execution prices rather than real-time mark-to-market consensus.

Consequently, disclosures submitted to regulatory bodies like the SEC offer rare visibility into how different institutional managers price identical private issuers. When funds report starkly different valuation points, it highlights the structural fragmentation inherent in secondary markets for private venture shares, particularly within the evolving cryptocurrency and financial technology sectors.
