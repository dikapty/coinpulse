# On-Chain Audit Shows Bitquery Discrepancy in Maple's Disclosed Bitcoin Wallets

Questions regarding protocol transparency and collateral tracking have re-emerged following an independent analysis of decentralized lending provider Maple. On November 18, 2026, blockchain data platform Bitquery performed a balance check across Maple's publicly disclosed wallet addresses. The investigation revealed a discrepancy between the total amount of Bitcoin (BTC) identified on-chain by Bitquery and the balance figures reported by Maple.

According to reporting by [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/bitquery-counted-400-fewer-btc-in-maples-disclosed-wallets-than-maple-reported/), Bitquery’s balance check counted 400 fewer BTC within Maple’s disclosed wallet infrastructure than the protocol had reported. This comparative audit brings renewed attention to how decentralized credit marketplaces verify, report, and maintain public records of their underlying assets. ## Understanding the Bitquery Discrepancy and Wallet Accounting

The gap identified by Bitquery highlights the operational complexities of relying on self-reported wallet disclosures versus independent on-chain verification. When examining the addresses provided in Maple's public disclosures, Bitquery's checking procedures accounted for 97.6% of the overall Bitcoin reported by the platform. The remaining fraction represents the 400 BTC difference between Maple's stated balance and the assets verified by Bitquery on-chain.

While a 400 BTC shortfall represents a noticeable numerical gap in reported statistics, the on-chain data still confirmed substantial backing behind Maple's operational loans. Based on the assets located during the November 18, 2026 check, Bitquery estimated that the counted collateral covered approximately 152% of Maple's active outstanding loans. This over-collateralization ratio demonstrates that despite the tracking discrepancy across disclosed addresses, the verifiable funds located on-chain remained well above the total value of the debt issued by the platform.

## Collateral Ratios and On-Chain Verification Dynamics

In decentralized credit markets, over-collateralization serves as a primary risk mitigation mechanism. Lenders rely on transparent collateral ratios to ensure that loans remain properly capitalized even during periods of broader market volatility. Bitquery’s finding that counted collateral stood at roughly 152% of total loans suggests that Maple's active lending pools maintain a buffer above a 1:1 debt-to-collateral ratio.

However, the presence of a 400 BTC difference between reported figures and verifiable wallet balances underscores persistent friction in on-chain reporting. Differences of this nature can stem from a variety of operational factors in institutional lending, including standard reporting lag times, unindexed operational wallets, off-chain liquidity management, or moving assets between intermediate custody addresses. Until protocols unify real-time on-chain accounting with public dashboard reporting, third-party analytics firms like Bitquery play a critical role in highlighting potential discrepancies for market participants.

## Implications for Decentralized Lending Protocols

The findings regarding Maple come at a time when decentralized finance platforms face growing demands for complete auditability. Institutional lenders and capital providers increasingly expect real-time verification of reserves rather than static or periodic disclosures. When third-party auditors identify uncounted or misaligned balances, it emphasizes the necessity for automated, continuous proof-of-reserves mechanisms.

For Maple and similar credit marketplaces, addressing variance between internal accounting and external wallet monitors is essential for maintaining liquidity provider confidence. Third-party checks, such as the one conducted by Bitquery on November 18, 2026, showcase how transparent blockchain architectures allow the public to independently evaluate asset coverage ratios. As verification tools become more sophisticated, protocols will likely face tighter scrutiny regarding how disclosures are formatted, updated, and cross-referenced against active mainnet wallets.
