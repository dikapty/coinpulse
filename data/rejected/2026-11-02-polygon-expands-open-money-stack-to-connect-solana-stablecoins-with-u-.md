# Polygon Expands Open Money Stack to Connect Solana Stablecoins with U.S. Banking Rails

Polygon has expanded its Open Money Stack (OMS) infrastructure to support Solana’s $15 billion stablecoin market. Announced on November 2, 2026, the integration allows eligible non-U.S. businesses to connect Solana-based stablecoins directly to traditional U.S. banking infrastructure following mandatory identity and verification procedures.

This cross-chain deployment brings institutional settlement infrastructure to Solana ecosystem participants while leveraging Polygon's enterprise liquidity rails. However, immediate availability remains gated under an early-access onboarding model and is restricted by specific regulatory and geographic boundaries.

## Bridging Decentralized Networks to U.S. Banking Rails

The expansion of Polygon’s OMS targets a persistent structural challenge within the digital asset industry: establishing efficient pathways between decentralized blockchain networks and legacy fiat clearing systems. Prior to this integration, commercial entities operating on Solana often relied on fragmented pathways to convert high-volume stablecoin holdings into fiat balances within institutional bank accounts.

According to coverage from [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/polygon-brings-us-banking-access-to-solanas-15-billion-stablecoin-market/), the newly introduced feature allows verified foreign commercial entities to bridge their asset flows directly into U.S. commercial bank rails. By providing this software connection, Polygon aims to streamline institutional operations, treasury management, and cross-border commercial settlements across the $15 billion stablecoin market active on the Solana network.

## Regulatory Controls and Onboarding Constraints

Despite the broader reach into the Solana ecosystem, access to the Open Money Stack is strictly controlled. Polygon has implemented mandatory identity and business verification steps that non-U.S. companies must complete prior to establishing a connection with the banking rails.

The service operates under defined geographic and jurisdictional limits. Polygon's rollout utilizes an early-access framework, restricting immediate participation to selected entities located within approved jurisdictions. These compliance mechanisms are designed to align with international standards for anti-money laundering (AML) and know-your-customer (KYC) regulations, ensuring that fiat conversion channels conform to applicable banking laws.

This is not financial advice. Market participants and institutional users should evaluate regional regulatory requirements and perform independent due diligence before utilizing cross-chain banking services.

## Interoperability and Institutional Utility

The deployment underscores a broader strategic focus among major blockchain developers on cross-chain infrastructure and real-world financial utility. Rather than competing exclusively for native on-chain liquidity, network operators are increasingly constructing unified tools that serve digital asset workflows regardless of the underlying chain.

By extending its banking tools into Solana’s stablecoin ecosystem, Polygon positions its Open Money Stack as a cross-chain settlement framework for decentralized finance (DeFi) and institutional commercial applications. For Solana, gaining direct access to U.S. banking rails offers enhanced liquidity pathways for enterprise users and international business operations running on its blockchain.

As the deployment moves through its initial early-access phase, the rollout will serve as a test case for how hybrid blockchain-to-banking software can scale across international regulatory regimes while addressing enterprise demand for stablecoin liquidity.
