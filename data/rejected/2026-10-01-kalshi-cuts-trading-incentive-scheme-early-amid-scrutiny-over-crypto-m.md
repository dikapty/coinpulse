# Kalshi Cuts Trading Incentive Scheme Early Amid Scrutiny Over Crypto Market Volume

Prediction-market operator Kalshi is ending a key trader-volume incentive program nearly a year ahead of schedule, following heightened scrutiny surrounding trading activity within its cryptocurrency markets. According to a Sept. 28 regulatory filing submitted to the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC), the company announced that its Volume Incentive Program will terminate no earlier than Oct. 13.

According to reportage by [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/kalshis-40-billion-growth-story-now-comes-with-a-harder-question-about-its-trading-volume/), the unexpected wind-down of the promotion marks a notable shift for the platform, which has built a $40 billion growth story around its rapidly expanding prediction markets. The decision directly addresses growing inquiries from market observers and regulators regarding how much of the trading activity on its platform represents organic user demand versus volume driven by artificial financial incentives.

## Pressure Mounts Over Prediction Market Trading Volume

The decision to pull back the incentive scheme follows intensifying public and regulatory focus on prediction platforms. While these venues have gained significant traction by allowing users to trade on the outcomes of real-world events, crypto asset prices, and economic indicators, critics have frequently raised questions about underlying liquidity and volume metrics.

Volume incentive programs are traditionally designed to bootstrap liquidity on financial exchanges by offering rebates, fee discounts, or tokenized rewards to high-frequency traders and market makers. However, when applied to retail-focused prediction markets, these mechanisms can obscure true platform adoption by encouraging wash trading or automated circular transactions executed solely to capture rewards. Kalshi’s early termination of its scheme suggests a deliberate effort to align its platform metrics with regulatory expectations as the CFTC maintains oversight over regulated derivatives exchanges.

## Regulatory Implications for Regulated Event Contracts

Kalshi operates as a Designated Contract Market (DCM) supervised by the CFTC, setting it apart from unregulated or offshore crypto prediction venues. Because of this formal regulatory status, any promotional program or trading contract offered by the platform is subject to reporting requirements and regulatory standards designed to prevent market manipulation and ensure transparent price discovery.

By formally notifying the CFTC of the early termination of the Volume Incentive Program, Kalshi is taking steps to alter its marketplace structure under direct regulatory visibility. Observers note that while cutting the incentive program may lead to a short-term drop in reported trading volumes across its crypto contracts, establishing organic, un-subsidized trading metrics is critical for long-term compliance and credibility in the United States financial ecosystem. ## Wider Shift in Crypto Market Liquidity and Oversight

The broader digital asset industry is navigating a multi-front shift in risk management, compliance, and regulatory enforcement. Beyond prediction markets, liquidity flows across alternative crypto venues are shifting under pressure from law enforcement and regulatory scrutiny. For instance, recent reports from [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/bitgets-hackers-turn-to-zcash-after-50-million-laundering-route-gets-blocked/) reveal that illicit actors from a $387.5 million breach at exchange Bitget recently diverted funds into privacy coin Zcash's Ironwood shielded pool after standard laundering routes totaling $50 million were blocked by compliance tracking across mainstream platforms.

Similarly, macroeconomic conditions continue to influence broader market sentiment and retail investment activity. Federal Reserve officials remain vigilant regarding inflationary pressures; Minneapolis Fed President Neel Kashkari noted in an interview with [CNBC Finance](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/30/watch-minneapolis-fed-president-neel-kashkari.html) that inflation remains "still too high" despite softer-than-expected PCE data, even as the labor market stays "pretty good." At the same time, policy changes outside of pure finance are impacting corporate operations, such as recent court actions reported by [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/second-judge-blocks-trumps-100000-fee-for-new-h1b-worker-visas-4926131) where a second federal judge blocked a proposed $100,000 fee for new H-1B worker visas.

## Outlook for Prediction Platform Business Models

As Kalshi prepares to shut down its incentive program on or after Oct. 13, the prediction market industry faces a crucial test. Platform operators must demonstrate that their market models can maintain sustainable liquidity, narrow bid-ask spreads, and attract active market participants without relying on balance-sheet subsidies or promotional trading programs.

Whether Kalshi can preserve its market leadership and maintain its $40 billion growth narrative under strictly organic volume will be closely watched by rival exchanges, institutional liquidity providers, and regulatory authorities alike. The transition marks a maturing phase for event-based derivatives, forcing the sector to prioritize transaction integrity and true market depth over promotional top-line growth metrics.
