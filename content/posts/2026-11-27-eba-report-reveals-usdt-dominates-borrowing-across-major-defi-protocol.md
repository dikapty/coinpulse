# EBA Report Reveals USDT Dominates Borrowing Across Major DeFi Protocols

Europe's banking regulator, the European Banking Authority (EBA), has highlighted the need for financial supervisors to assess and strengthen safeguards surrounding cryptocurrency firms that direct retail and institutional clients into decentralized finance (DeFi) lending protocols. The recommendation stems from an examination conducted by the authority into decentralized borrowing protocols, which revealed that Tether’s USDT stablecoin serves as the primary borrowed asset across the majority of the platforms evaluated.

According to findings detailed by [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/usdt-leads-borrowing-on-four-of-six-defi-protocols-examined-by-europes-banking-watchdog/), Tether (USDT) leads borrowing activity on four out of six major DeFi protocols analyzed by the European banking supervisory body. The data underscores the central role that USD-pegged stablecoins play in providing liquidity and leverage within non-custodial financial protocols, drawing regulatory focus toward consumer protection, systemic risk, and supervisory oversight.

## The Role of USDT in Decentralized Lending Protocols

Decentralized lending protocols utilize smart contracts to execute automated peer-to-pool borrowing and lending without central intermediaries. Participants typically deposit supported digital assets as collateral to borrow stablecoins or alternative digital tokens. The examination by the European regulator shows that USDT is the most frequently borrowed asset on four of the six examined platforms.

This concentration of USDT borrowing reflects broader digital asset market trends where stablecoins serve as primary settlement mediums and dollar proxies for trading and leverage. Because USDT is widely deployed to facilitate rapid liquidity access across multiple blockchain networks, its position in lending protocols illustrates how centralized stablecoin assets are integrated into decentralized systems.

However, this reliance also presents concentrated market exposure. When centralized crypto brokers or intermediaries route customer funds into decentralized lending setups, users can be exposed to operational and structural risks—such as smart contract code vulnerabilities, automated liquidations caused by price volatility, and unmonitored operational practices—that fall outside conventional banking regulatory protections.

## Supervisory Focus on Intermediaries and Client Routing

European financial supervisors are placing increased emphasis on the links connecting regulated centralized entities with permissionless decentralized applications. The watchdog’s recommendations target crypto intermediaries, emphasizing the potential risks created when brokers, portals, or platforms direct retail or institutional clients toward decentralized lending protocols.

While permissionless smart contracts operate autonomously on public blockchains, centralized entities, hosted portals, wallet providers, and exchanges offering access to these mechanisms remain subject to supervisory evaluation. Regulatory authorities express concern that users may not fully distinguish between holding assets with a regulated financial institution and placing capital into automated smart contracts.

By documenting that USDT leads borrowing on two-thirds of the analyzed protocol sample, the supervisory body indicates that monitoring liquidity concentrations and gateway routing practices will remain a regulatory priority moving forward.

## Regulatory Environment and Market Safeguards

This supervisory focus aligns with ongoing efforts across European jurisdictions to implement consistent oversight of digital asset ecosystems. As frameworks like the Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA) regulation take effect across the European Union, regional supervisors are closely monitoring stablecoin reserves, issuer compliance standards, and secondary market access points.

The regulator's examination of decentralized borrowing suggests that rules targeting stablecoin issuers alone may not address all systemic considerations if the primary deployment of those tokens occurs within permissionless DeFi pools. Regulators remain concerned about market contagion risks, where abrupt market drops, stablecoin de-pegging events, or protocol-level exploits could spread across connected centralized and decentralized platforms.

Entities operating within European jurisdictions may encounter stricter disclosure obligations, suitability evaluations, and operational guidelines if they provide customer access to third-party decentralized lending protocols. ## Evaluating Innovation and Risk Management

Supervisors face an ongoing task in balancing technology innovation with market integrity and consumer safeguards. Decentralized lending offers automated execution, public blockchain transaction verification, and continuous operation without standard credit checks. Nevertheless, the absence of traditional legal recourse in permissionless protocols remains a key topic of discussion for institutional risk managers and regulators.

The findings from the European banking regulator highlight a shift in supervisory attention toward analyzing practical asset usage patterns. As stablecoins like USDT remain dominant liquidity vehicles in automated lending protocols, European regulatory bodies are expected to seek further clarity on client access rules, risk disclosures, and operational safeguards across digital asset service providers.
