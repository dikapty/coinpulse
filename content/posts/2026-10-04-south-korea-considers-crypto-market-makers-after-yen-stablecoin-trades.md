# South Korea Considers Crypto Market Makers After Yen Stablecoin Trades Four Times Above Peg

South Korean financial authorities are re-evaluating their regulatory restrictions on cryptocurrency market makers following severe price volatility on domestic trading platforms. The policy review was triggered after the Japanese yen-pegged stablecoin, JPYC, experienced a dramatic price spike on the local exchange Upbit, trading at four times its intended peg value.

Currently, cryptocurrency market-making operates under tight legal constraints in South Korea due to strict regulations designed to curb market manipulation. However, the recent price distortion has highlighted structural vulnerabilities caused by thin liquidity on local order books, prompting regulators to assess whether legal market makers are necessary to ensure stable trading environments.

## Disruption in Stablecoin Peg Exposes Domestic Liquidity Gaps

According to reporting by [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/south-korea-weighs-crypto-market-makers-after-jpyc-trades-at-4-times-peg?utm_source=rss_feed&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=rss_partner_inbound), the regulatory re-examination began after trading activity on Upbit caused JPYC to detach completely from its baseline fiat reference. Stablecoins are engineered to maintain a predictable valuation tied to an underlying asset. When JPYC surged to four times its designated value, it demonstrated how localized supply shortages and a lack of liquidity provision can result in severe market imbalances on South Korean exchanges.

Under South Korea's existing regulatory regime, activities typical of market makers—such as continuously placing buy and sell quotes to deepen order books and minimize price slippage—are largely restricted. Financial regulators have historically taken a cautious stance, treating continuous liquidity provision with suspicion due to concerns over potential market manipulation. Consequently, local order books remain vulnerable to rapid price spikes whenever unmanaged buying or selling pressure enters the market.

## Balancing Market Integrity and Manipulation Restrictions

Financial regulators in South Korea face a complex challenge as they weigh the formal introduction of authorized crypto market makers. As noted by [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/south-korea-weighs-crypto-market-makers-after-jpyc-trades-at-4-times-peg?utm_source=rss_feed&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=rss_partner_inbound), current legal constraints were originally established to prevent predatory practices such as wash trading, spoofing, and artificial volume inflation. However, completely excluding professional liquidity providers leaves retail investors exposed to extreme slippage and abnormal pricing structures during localized supply shocks.

By reconsidering their stance, regulators are exploring whether clear legal frameworks for market making can exist alongside strict anti-manipulation enforcement. Establishing institutional parameters would allow designated entities to legally supply liquidity and absorb order flow. This structural shift could prevent localized exchange prices from drifting drastically away from broader global market valuations.

## Altcoin Volume Expansion Surges Across Digital Asset Markets

The discussions in South Korea regarding market stability coincide with broader shifts in global trading behavior across alternative digital assets. As reported by [CryptoPotato](https://cryptopotato.com/altcoin-trading-volume-hits-4x-bitcoin-as-one-trader-says-hes-out/), recent market metrics show that altcoin trading volume has surged to four times that of Bitcoin. This dramatic increase highlights elevated speculative activity and heightened participation in alternative token markets.

The massive inflow into altcoins has led some market analysts and traders to alter their risk management strategies. For example, a popular trader known as Doctor Profit announced that he is taking profits and exiting positions, citing concerns over market overheating and potential downside risks as altcoin volumes reach disproportionate levels relative to Bitcoin. When speculative activity accelerates in lower-liquidity assets or segregated domestic markets, the probability of erratic price spikes and sudden flash crashes increases significantly.

## Regulatory Context and Long-Term Market Impact

South Korea's potential regulatory shift represents a critical moment for domestic trading platforms like Upbit. Allowing professional market makers to operate under official supervision could significantly improve order book depth and help align domestic prices with global benchmarks. Historically, South Korean exchanges have experienced localized price discrepancies—often referred to as regional premiums or discounts—due to capital controls and restricted market access.

While South Korean financial authorities continue to evaluate potential policy adjustments, any finalized framework is expected to incorporate strict compliance, monitoring, and transparency standards to ensure liquidity providers operate without engaging in manipulative practices. For now, the JPYC price disruption serves as a key catalyst for policy discussions, demonstrating that an absolute restriction on liquidity provision can inadvertently generate the very volatility regulators seek to avoid.
