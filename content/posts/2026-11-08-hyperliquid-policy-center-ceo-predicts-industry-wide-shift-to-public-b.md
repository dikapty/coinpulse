# Hyperliquid Policy Center CEO Predicts Industry-Wide Shift to Public Blockchain Infrastructure

As of November 8, 2026, the intersection of decentralized finance and traditional regulatory frameworks continues to undergo a significant evolution. Jake Chervinsky, CEO of the Hyperliquid Policy Center, has articulated a vision for the future of crypto exchanges, asserting that the shift toward public blockchain infrastructure is not merely an optional upgrade but an eventual necessity for survival in a competitive landscape. According to [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/regulation/2026-10-07-hyperliquid-policy-center-jake-chervinsky-interview-417896), Chervinsky believes that every exchange will be compelled to adopt this technological standard to maintain market viability.

The core of Chervinsky’s argument centers on the modernization of market operations. While the industry has historically relied on centralized, opaque order books, the move toward public, on-chain infrastructure promises a level of transparency that regulators and institutional participants are increasingly demanding. Beyond the current prevalence of regulated perpetual futures, Chervinsky highlights that on-chain markets must actively integrate into the established U.S. regulatory perimeter. This integration is expected to occur in the near term, bridging the gap between decentralized innovation and established legal oversight. By leveraging public blockchains, exchanges can provide verifiable proof of reserves and execution, potentially mitigating the systemic risks associated with centralized entities. ## The Strategic Rationale for On-Chain Markets

The move toward on-chain transparency represents a maturation of the infrastructure supporting digital asset trading. For years, the crypto exchange sector operated with limited visibility into the clearing and settlement processes that define traditional finance. As regulatory bodies become more sophisticated in their approach to digital assets, the ability to demonstrate compliance through the blockchain itself offers a streamlined path to legitimacy. Chervinsky’s assertion that public infrastructure is the inevitable destination for all exchanges suggests that the industry is moving away from private, proprietary ledgers and toward a shared, immutable standard.

This shift is particularly relevant as the regulatory environment becomes more rigorous. If exchanges host their order matching and settlement on-chain, they provide a layer of accountability that differs from traditional "black box" matching engines. This transparency satisfies potential regulatory inquiries and empowers participants to audit market conditions in real time. The focus on integrating these markets into the U.S. regulatory perimeter is a critical indicator of where the industry is heading: the goal is not to circumvent regulation, but to build a system natively designed to function within it. This pivot toward institutional-grade infrastructure prioritizes transparency and verifiable safety measures.

## Global Context and Market Dynamics

The conversation surrounding market integrity extends beyond the United States. Recent events in other jurisdictions highlight why this push for standardized, reliable infrastructure is pressing. As reported by [CoinTelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/south-korea-weighs-crypto-market-makers-after-jpyc-trades-at-4-times-peg?utm_source=rss_feed&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=rss_partner_inbound) on September 28, 2026, regulators in South Korea have been reconsidering their stance on crypto market makers. This internal review was triggered by extreme price volatility where a JPYC token spiked to four times its intended peg on the Upbit exchange. Because South Korea’s current rules effectively restrict active market making to prevent manipulation, the lack of sufficient liquidity providers left the market vulnerable to massive price distortions.

This incident illustrates the risks inherent in fragmented or immature infrastructure. When exchanges lack the depth provided by efficient market makers, or rely on proprietary systems that cannot handle stress, the risk to retail and institutional users increases. By shifting toward the public blockchain architecture advocated by Chervinsky, the industry could theoretically address liquidity and transparency issues on a global scale. Public, on-chain order books allow for automated, transparent market making that is visible to participants, potentially reducing the impact of decoupling events. The objective is to transition from a system relying on restrictive, reactive policy toward one that uses technology to enforce stability and fair play.

## Future Implications for Exchange Operators

The transition envisioned by the Hyperliquid Policy Center is expected to be a multi-year effort that reshapes the competitive dynamics of the crypto space. Exchanges that resist the move toward public blockchain infrastructure may face disadvantages regarding regulatory acceptance and user trust. The ability to verify transactions and order flows on-chain is becoming a baseline requirement for institutional investors, who remain hesitant to engage with platforms that offer less transparency than traditional stock exchanges.

As the industry looks toward 2027 and beyond, the focus will likely remain on integrating decentralized protocols into the broader financial system. Whether through the adoption of public ledger matching engines or the creation of hybrid systems that maintain high speed while ensuring public auditability, the path toward transparency is clear. For investors and market participants, this evolution marks a departure from earlier eras of crypto toward a future where trading platforms function with a level of clarity and accountability previously unseen in the digital asset domain. While specific regulatory outcomes remain to be seen, the technological trend favors open, on-chain solutions that satisfy the requirements of the market and authorities.
