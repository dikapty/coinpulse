# Bitcoin Drops to Four-Month Low Near $61,000 Amid $820 Million Liquidation Wave and Record ETF Withdrawals

Bitcoin traded down to its lowest price level in four months on November 20, 2026, as a massive wave of derivatives liquidations coincided with historically high institutional capital outflows. Market data shows that the leading cryptocurrency dropped to $61,196 earlier in the day before experiencing a slight rebound to trade at $61,901, according to reporting by [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/bitcoin-hits-four-month-low-as-820-million-liquidated-and-12-7-billion-short-squeeze-risk-builds/).

The move extends a persistent downward trajectory across the digital asset market that has repeatedly punished leveraged traders attempting to call a bottom. Over the course of the market sell-off, roughly $820 million in position liquidations swept through crypto derivatives venues. The sharp decline in spot prices leaves the market exposed to heightened volatility, even as derivative metrics signal potential structural imbalances forming across trading positions.

## Institutional Withdrawals Accelerate Spot Bitcoin ETF Outflows

Compounding the pressure seen on derivative exchanges, institutional investors have been paring back exposure to spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds (ETFs) in the United States at a near-record pace. According to data from Farside Investors reported by [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/blackrock-leads-582-million-bitcoin-etf-exodus-as-november-withdrawals-approach-3-5-billion/), total net outflows for U.S. spot Bitcoin ETFs reached $3.47 billion through November 19, 2026.

This monthly net outflow figure puts the current period roughly $1.04 billion short of the historical record set in June, when total net redemptions peaked higher. The withdrawal activity highlights a significant pivot in institutional sentiment during November, reversing previous inflows into regulated investment products. The sustained redemptions from spot ETFs have removed substantial capital from the market, putting added pressure on spot pricing relative to historical levels.

## BlackRock IBIT Registers Record Single-Day Net Outflows

The institutional exodus was heavily driven by redemptions from the market’s largest spot fund. BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) recorded $541 million in net outflows on November 19, marking its single largest day of net redemptions since its market launch in January 2024, as cited by [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/blackrock-leads-582-million-bitcoin-etf-exodus-as-november-withdrawals-approach-3-5-billion/).

The $541 million single-day outflow surpassed IBIT's previous daily record outflow of $528 million. Prior to this sell-off period, the BlackRock fund had largely acted as the primary vehicle for net inflows within the U.S. spot ETF ecosystem. The massive withdrawal from a premier spot vehicle demonstrates how quickly institutional allocation strategies can shift during broader risk-off movements, directly impacting spot market equilibrium.

## Derivatives Market Liquidations and Building Short Squeeze Risks

The sharp drop to the $61,196 level wiped out $820 million worth of leveraged positions across derivative exchanges, primarily affecting long traders positioned for a price recovery. However, the aggressive push downward by short sellers has altered the underlying structural conditions in the futures and options markets.

Market analysis published by [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/bitcoin-hits-four-month-low-as-820-million-liquidated-and-12-7-billion-short-squeeze-risk-builds/) indicates that as Bitcoin's price performance extended its sell-off, a short squeeze risk valued at $12.7 billion has gradually accumulated. If spot prices recover unexpectedly, short traders who entered positions during the breakdown toward four-month lows may be forced to buy back contracts to cover their positions. This dynamic creates the potential for sharp upward price spikes despite the dominant bearish market trends seen throughout November.

## Market Context and Broader Financial Implications

The combined impact of spot ETF outflows approaching historical records and massive derivatives liquidations underscores the growing interconnectedness between traditional institutional finance and native crypto derivatives markets. Because institutional investors trade spot ETFs while derivative market participants utilize leverage, sell-offs in one domain can trigger cascading liquidations in the other.

Traders and institutional market participants are paying close attention to whether spot ETF outflows will breach the previous record set in June, or if current pricing near $61,901 will establish a temporary base. As financial products like spot ETFs continue to mature alongside leveraged derivative markets, sudden shifts in capital allocations remain a key driver of asset price fluctuations.
