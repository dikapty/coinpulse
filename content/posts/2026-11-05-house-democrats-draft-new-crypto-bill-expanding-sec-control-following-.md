## House Democrats Draft New Crypto Framework After CLARITY Act Collapse

House Democrats are developing a comprehensive cryptocurrency market structure bill designed to expand the regulatory authority of the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC). The legislative initiative follows the recent collapse of the CLARITY Act, marking a renewed push by congressional lawmakers to establish clear federal oversight mechanisms for the digital asset industry.

According to details outlined in a November 4 social media post by Fox Business journalist Eleanor Terrett, the emerging legislative framework draws heavily from earlier investor-rights proposals originally introduced by Representative Maxine Waters. Discussions among Democratic committee staff indicate a intent to fundamentally shift how federal agencies divide supervisory responsibilities over the digital asset ecosystem.

Under the proposed framework, the SEC would receive a dominant regulatory mandate over digital assets, while the authority assigned to the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) would be scaled back. This shift represents a significant departure from alternative legislative proposals that sought to empower the CFTC as the primary regulator for digital commodity spot markets.

## Rebalancing Authority Between the SEC and CFTC

The jurisdiction split between the SEC and the CFTC has long been a major focal point for digital asset firms, market venues, and legal observers. The classification of a digital token as either a security or a commodity dictates the specific compliance burdens, disclosure requirements, and operational rules imposed on industry participants.

Proposals favoring primary CFTC oversight have generally drawn support from industry advocates who view the commodities framework as better tailored to spot market trading. Conversely, frameworks anchored in SEC jurisdiction subject token issuers, trading platforms, and market intermediaries to strict registration demands, comprehensive public reporting standards, and investor protection rules modeled on traditional equity and debt markets.

According to sources familiar with the discussions who met with Democratic staff, the new proposal aims to institute broader market structure guidelines in the wake of previous legislative stalemates. By leveraging Representative Waters' prior investor-focused legislation, the draft prioritizes consumer protection mechanisms and regulatory enforcement over industry-preferred regulatory carve-outs.

## Heightened Ethics Rules and Regulatory Scope

In addition to reallocating agency jurisdiction, the draft legislation seeks to incorporate stricter government ethics restrictions. These measures target the interactions between public officials, regulatory agency personnel, and the financial technology sector, reflecting heightened legislative scrutiny over potential conflicts of interest in market oversight.

The inclusion of expanded ethics mandates signals that lawmakers intend to address governance standards alongside technical market mechanics. How these ethics provisions are drafted will likely form a key area of debate as the bill moves into formal committee drafting and legislative hearings.

The strategic shift by Democratic lawmakers comes directly after the breakdown of the CLARITY Act, a bill previously viewed as a potential path toward statutory regulatory clarity. The failure of the CLARITY Act left market participants without definitive legislative rules regarding exchange registration, token categorization, or statutory agency boundaries.

## Market Impact and Legislative Outlook

Rather than pausing legislative efforts following the CLARITY Act's failure, House Democratic staff pivoted immediately to building an alternative model. The choice to centralize oversight within the SEC aligns with persistent calls from consumer protection advocates who argue that existing securities laws provide the most effective framework for safeguarding retail investors against market manipulation and platform insolvencies.

As Democratic staff refine the draft text, market participants are monitoring several critical provisions. Key areas of focus include explicit definitions for digital asset classifications, proposed transition periods for operating platforms to achieve full compliance, and exact statutory language defining exchange operations.
