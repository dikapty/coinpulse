# Nasdaq CEO Adena Friedman Highlights Tokenization as Catalyst to Unleash Tens of Billions in Trapped Capital

In a major assessment of how blockchain technology is reshaping the global financial architecture, Nasdaq CEO Adena Friedman stated that tokenization could free up tens of billions of dollars in trapped capital. The comments, shared during a panel discussion at the Sibos financial conference in Beijing, underscore a growing consensus among traditional financial leaders that digital asset technology is moving beyond speculative trading and entering a phase of deep institutional integration. 

As major financial institutions look for ways to optimize liquidity and streamline operations, the digitization of real-world assets (RWA) has emerged as a primary focus. Friedman's remarks highlight how moving traditional assets onto distributed ledgers can eliminate structural friction, accelerate settlement times, and unlock vast pools of capital that are currently locked up in inefficient settlement pipelines and collateral registries.

## Understanding the Mechanics of Trapped Capital

To understand why tokenization is attracting serious attention from market infrastructure giants like Nasdaq, it is necessary to examine how capital becomes "trapped" in the legacy financial system. Under current market structures, transactions involving various financial assets require complex chains of intermediaries to clear and settle. According to Friedman, capital is trapped in the system because of the time required to complete settlements.

This delayed settlement cycle—which can span multiple days in traditional global markets—requires financial institutions to maintain substantial collateral buffers to cover risk during the settlement period. This capital is essentially immobilized, earning minimal returns and remaining unavailable for more productive investments. By converting these assets into digital tokens on a blockchain, transaction clearing and settlement can happen almost instantaneously. Programmable technology can automate compliance and transfer mechanisms, thereby reducing the necessity for massive collateral buffers and freeing up those assets for more efficient use.

## The Institutional Shift and the Role of Central Bank Money

Nasdaq's public interest in tokenization is part of a broader, systemic shift across Wall Street and global financial hubs. Traditional exchanges and custodian banks are increasingly looking at digital asset technology to cater to institutional demand. The objective is to bring the efficiency of blockchain technology to traditional financial instruments, including debt securities, cash, and physical assets.

However, Friedman noted that for tokenization to reach its full scale, the industry requires "digital cash" to settle transactions. While tokenizing a bond or another asset is a significant step, the settlement process still needs a digital cash leg to complete the transaction seamlessly. Friedman expressed a preference for this digital cash leg to be backed by central bank money, which would provide the highest level of trust and safety for institutional participants, rather than relying solely on private stablecoins or other private digital currencies. 

## Regulatory Hurdles and Technical Standards

Despite the significant financial benefits outlined by Friedman, widespread adoption of tokenization faces several critical bottlenecks. Chief among these is the lack of a unified global regulatory framework. Financial institutions operate across multiple jurisdictions, each with its own set of rules governing digital signatures, smart contracts, and asset custody. For tokenization to truly free up tens of billions of dollars globally, regulators must establish clear guidelines that recognize blockchain-based ownership as legally binding.

Furthermore, Friedman emphasized the need for legal and regulatory certainty, particularly regarding how ownership is recorded. For institutional adoption to succeed, the industry needs a clear legal definition of what constitutes a "record of ownership" on a blockchain. Without this foundation, institutions cannot confidently migrate high-value assets to distributed ledgers. Additionally, establishing common industry standards is critical to prevent technological fragmentation, ensuring that different blockchain networks and legacy systems can communicate with each other seamlessly.

## The Broadening Digital Asset Payment Ecosystem

The momentum behind institutional digital assets extends beyond the tokenization of securities. The broader financial ecosystem is concurrently adopting digital payment systems to facilitate commercial transactions. For instance, enterprise software provider SAP is integrating stablecoin payments directly into corporate workflows [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/saps-new-usdc-payments-require-an-eligible-circle-mint-account/). Through its SAP Pay platform, the company is testing digital dollar payments to allow immediate, programmable settlement of business-to-business transactions, helping corporate clients bypass slow, expensive international bank transfers.

To access these payment features, corporate users must link an eligible Circle Mint account, enabling them to transact using USDC [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/saps-new-usdc-payments-require-an-eligible-circle-mint-account/). This integration demonstrates how digital currencies are beginning to bridge the gap between traditional enterprise software and decentralized financial rails. As these payment rails and corporate digital wallets mature, they will provide the practical transactional infrastructure to support the large-scale settlement of tokenized traditional capital assets.

## Deep Liquidity and Market Capitalization Dynamics

As institutions explore the tokenization of assets, market participants are also developing a more sophisticated understanding of digital asset liquidity and supply dynamics. In major token markets, researchers are increasingly distinguishing between total circulating supply and the actual executable supply available on exchanges [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/xrp-has-63-billion-circulating-tokens-but-how-many-are-actually-for-sale/).

For example, in the market for XRP, while the total circulating supply is approximately 63 billion tokens, a significant portion of this supply is held in long-term wallets, escrow accounts, or retail holdings that are not actively for sale [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/xrp-has-63-billion-circulating-tokens-but-how-many-are-actually-for-sale/). Only a small fraction of the circulating supply represents executable liquidity on exchanges [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/xrp-has-63-billion-circulating-tokens-but-how-many-are-actually-for-sale/). This distinction between nominal market capitalization and real, actionable market depth is critical for institutional investors who require deep liquidity to enter and exit positions without causing extreme price volatility. Understanding these liquidity structures will be essential as more traditional assets are tokenized and traded on digital venues.
