# Bitget Resumes Bitcoin Withdrawals After $387.5 Million Hack as Outflows Surge

On September 28, 2026, cryptocurrency exchange Bitget reopened withdrawals following a devastating $387.5 million security breach. The resumption of services immediately triggered a significant outflow of assets, with nearly 5,000 Bitcoin (BTC) leaving the exchange's tracked reserves in a matter of hours. The massive movement of funds comes as blockchain security analysts observe hackers beginning to launder the multimillion-dollar haul stolen during the exploit.

According to an update from Bitget Chief Executive Officer Gracy Chen, the exchange processed 9,585 withdrawal orders totaling 4,098.036 BTC shortly after resuming Bitcoin withdrawals. The processing of these orders was completed by 17:00 UTC+8 on the day of the announcement. Meanwhile, separate data tracked by decentralized finance analytics platform DeFiLlama confirmed a sharp drop in Bitget's reserve balances as users rushed to secure their assets. 

## The Aftermath of the $387.5 Million Hack

The reopening of withdrawals marks a critical phase in the exchange's recovery process, though it has exposed the platform to immediate liquidity pressure. When a major cryptocurrency trading platform suffers a security breach of this magnitude, standard operating procedures typically involve freezing all deposit and withdrawal mechanisms to secure remaining assets, patch vulnerabilities, and conduct thorough forensic audits. 

However, the period directly following the reactivation of these services is often characterized by high transactional volume. Users, spooked by the initial exploit, frequently seek to migrate their holdings to external self-custody wallets or alternative trading venues. The departure of nearly 5,000 BTC from [Bitget's tracked reserves](https://cryptoslate.com/nearly-5000-btc-leaves-bitget-as-hackers-begin-laundering-387-million-haul/) highlights this common post-hack pattern. At the same time, the security landscape is further complicated by the actions of the attackers themselves. On-chain data indicates that the perpetrators behind the $387.5 million exploit have initiated laundering operations to obscure the origin of their illicitly acquired funds. Laundering processes in the decentralized ecosystem typically involve routing assets through various decentralized protocols, mixers, or intermediate addresses to sever the link to the original exploit.

## Broad Market Activity Amid Exchange Outflows

While Bitget manages its recovery and security enforcement, the broader cryptocurrency ecosystem continues to navigate shifting liquidity dynamics and institutional accumulation patterns. The movement of Bitcoin is not isolated to security-related incidents; institutional players are actively adjusting their portfolios. 

For example, MicroStrategy (recently renamed Strategy), led by Executive Chairman Michael Saylor, has signaled renewed confidence in the primary digital asset by executing back-to-back weekly Bitcoin purchases. This represents the company’s first consecutive weekly buy behavior since June 2026, marking a notable acceleration in accumulation after the firm sold a portion of its cryptocurrency holdings earlier in the quarter. On September 28, the company disclosed that it acquired 1,665 BTC for $142.7 million during the week ending September 27, paying an average price of $85,681 per token. 

These contrasting flows—significant outflows from a compromised exchange alongside aggressive programmatic buying by corporate treasuries—illustrate the complex, multi-layered nature of the current digital asset market. While retail and institutional depositors on Bitget prioritize custody security and risk mitigation, long-term corporate accumulators are leveraging market structures to expand their balance sheets. ## Security Challenges and the Path Forward

The breach at Bitget is part of a broader trend of persistent security threats facing the cryptocurrency sector. As platforms scale and manage larger pools of capital, they remain prime targets for sophisticated threat actors. The loss of $387.5 million places this incident among the more substantial exploits in recent memory, underscoring the ongoing vulnerabilities inherent in centralized exchange architectures and hot wallet management. 

According to [CryptoSlate's reporting](https://cryptoslate.com/nearly-5000-btc-leaves-bitget-as-hackers-begin-laundering-387-million-haul/), on-chain security tracking has already flagged the movement of stolen assets. This suggests the hackers are rapidly seeking to obfuscate their digital footprint before exchanges and law enforcement agencies can freeze the stolen funds on external platforms. For Bitget, the immediate priority remains the stabilization of its platform and the reassurance of its global user base.

Resolving nearly 10,000 pending withdrawal orders in a single day demonstrates an operational capacity to meet liquidity demands under stress, yet the long-term impact on the exchange's reputation and reserve depth remains to be seen. Security firms and on-chain analysts are expected to monitor the hackers' addresses closely as they attempt to disperse the stolen millions across the global blockchain network.
