# MSCI Finalizes Index Rule Changes: Crypto Treasury Firms Face Potential Exclusion

As of Friday, October 16, 2026, the global investment landscape is adjusting to a significant shift in market index construction. MSCI has officially finalized a set of new rules governing "non-operating companies," a development that carries potential consequences for publicly traded firms with substantial cryptocurrency holdings. These rule changes, which have been under close scrutiny by market participants, could result in the exclusion of notable companies—including Strategy, Metaplanet, and SharpLink—from influential stock market indexes.

The implications of these changes are broad, as inclusion in MSCI indexes often dictates the allocation strategies for institutional investors, passive index funds, and ETFs. By modifying the criteria for how non-operating companies are classified and included, MSCI is tightening the requirements for firms whose business models rely heavily on assets rather than traditional operational cash flows. The finalized list of companies impacted by these regulatory adjustments is scheduled for release on November 11, 2026. For investors and market analysts, this upcoming date serves as a critical checkpoint in determining which firms will retain their index status and which will be forced to adapt their treasury management strategies to remain eligible for future inclusion.

## The Impact of Treasury Management on Index Eligibility

At the heart of the controversy is the evolving definition of an "operating company." For years, traditional index providers like MSCI have evaluated companies based on their core revenue-generating activities. However, the rise of "crypto treasury" strategies—where corporations allocate a significant portion of their balance sheets to digital assets like Bitcoin—has complicated these classifications. Under the newly finalized rules, the criteria for non-operating companies have been refined to potentially categorize firms with high asset-to-operation ratios as being outside the standard scope for equity index inclusion.

This shift matters because institutional capital is highly sensitive to index membership. When a company is removed from an MSCI index, funds that track that index are often forced to sell their holdings in that firm, potentially leading to increased volatility and a decrease in liquidity for the excluded stock. For firms like Strategy, Metaplanet, and SharpLink, which have prominently adopted strategies focused on the accumulation of digital assets, this regulatory change is a tangible challenge. The industry is now waiting to see whether these companies will modify their treasury structures to comply with the new standards or accept the risk of potential exclusion from major benchmarks. This adjustment period highlights the tension between traditional index governance and corporate balance sheets increasingly dominated by volatile digital assets.

## Broadening the Regulatory Environment for Digital Assets

This development occurs against a backdrop of increasing global scrutiny regarding the intersection of traditional finance and digital assets. While MSCI focuses on index integrity, other jurisdictions are simultaneously formalizing their own regulatory frameworks. For example, as of October 2026, the European Union has made significant strides in the oversight of e-money tokens under the Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA) regulation. USDC, a prominent stablecoin, recently became the first of 45 MiCA-notified e-money tokens to achieve a major regulatory milestone in the EU. This suggests that while regulatory pressure is tightening in some areas—such as index inclusion—there is a concurrent push toward establishing clear, standardized frameworks for digital asset utility in Europe.

These events, while distinct in their focus, collectively illustrate a maturing market. The exclusion risk from MSCI indexes represents a "gatekeeper" function of traditional finance, while the MiCA compliance for stablecoins represents a pathway toward legitimate adoption in regulated markets. For investors, these shifts underscore the need for a comprehensive understanding of how regulatory decisions, whether at the level of index providers or national legislatures, can affect the valuation and accessibility of companies and assets. It remains to be seen how the specific list of excluded firms, due on November 11, 2026, will impact market sentiment and institutional allocation toward companies that view crypto as a foundational element of their corporate treasury strategy.

## Market Implications and Future Outlook

As the market prepares for the November 11 announcement, there is continued debate regarding whether the criteria used by index providers adequately capture the value of modern, technology-forward firms. Some argue that these rules are essential for maintaining the "operating" nature of index benchmarks, ensuring that they reflect traditional corporate health. Conversely, others argue that penalizing firms for their treasury choices could create an artificial barrier to innovation for companies operating at the intersection of crypto and traditional equity markets.

In the broader context of the digital asset space, market conditions remain fluid. For instance, the performance of assets like Pi Network (PI) continues to be the subject of predictive modeling, with some forecasts suggesting the potential for continued downward pressure through the end of 2026. Simultaneously, the long-term outlook for major assets like Bitcoin is being weighed against technical challenges, including the necessity for quantum-security upgrades. Industry experts, such as the CEO of Capriole, have highlighted that Bitcoin developers face a critical window of six to 12 months to address quantum threats, noting that a successful implementation of such security measures could significantly influence the asset's valuation. 

The convergence of these various factors—index rules, EU regulatory milestones, and technical security benchmarks—highlights the multifaceted nature of the current financial environment. As of October 16, 2026, market participants must navigate a landscape where regulatory compliance, index eligibility, and technical maturity are more intertwined than ever.
