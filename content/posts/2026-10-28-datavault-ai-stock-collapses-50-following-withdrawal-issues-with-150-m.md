# Datavault AI Stock Collapses 50% Following Withdrawal Issues With $150 Million Bitcoin Deal

Shares of Datavault AI experienced a sharp decline of 50% following a public disclosure that the firm is currently unable to withdraw Bitcoin associated with a $150 million transaction involving Scilex. The news, reported on October 27, 2026, details how the firm’s attempt to settle an equity deal through digital assets has led to significant operational and financial uncertainty [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-10-27-datavault-ai-stock-plummets-bitcoin-deal-431962). 

This event highlights the risks inherent in corporate treasury strategies that prioritize cryptocurrency for capital settlements. As public companies explore the integration of digital assets onto their balance sheets, the case of Datavault AI illustrates the potential for liquidity bottlenecks when those assets are not immediately accessible or transferable.

## The $150 Million Scilex Transaction and Liquidity Risks

The financial friction centers on a deal between Datavault AI and Scilex. According to the terms reported by [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/business/2026-10-27-datavault-ai-stock-plummets-bitcoin-deal-431962), Scilex intended to acquire $150 million in Datavault AI equity. The parties structured this arrangement to be settled directly in Bitcoin rather than through traditional fiat banking channels. 

Difficulties arose when Datavault AI confirmed it could not access or withdraw the Bitcoin designated for this transaction. Because the capital infusion was tied to this specific digital asset settlement, the inability to move the funds effectively froze the expected financial boost to the company’s balance sheet. For equity investors, the lack of immediate liquidity and the ambiguity surrounding the deal's finalization prompted a rapid sell-off. The 50% drop in share price reflects investor concern regarding the company's control over its assets and the viability of the underlying contract. This is not financial advice.

## Corporate Treasury and Operational Challenges

The Datavault AI situation serves as a case study for the structural risks firms encounter when opting for cryptocurrency in large-scale corporate finance. While digital assets are sometimes favored for their theoretical speed in settlement, they are subject to operational complexities—such as multi-signature management, reliance on third-party custodians, and technical barriers that do not exist within traditional fiat clearinghouses. 

When a standard cash-based equity transaction fails, the legal and financial frameworks are generally well-defined. By contrast, an operational failure in a crypto-denominated deal—specifically a withdrawal lock—can leave stakeholders without clear recourse. In the eyes of market observers, the inability to access corporate funds is a critical red flag. Until Datavault AI provides transparency regarding the nature of the withdrawal failure and the status of the $150 million, the company faces intense scrutiny regarding its asset management practices.

## Context Within the Broader Digital Asset Market

This decline occurred against a backdrop of broader activity in the digital asset sector. On October 28, 2026, market data indicated that Bitcoin was trading near $81,000 as investors awaited upcoming economic announcements from the Federal Reserve [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/10/28/bitcoin-nears-usd81-000-ahead-of-fed-decision-as-zec-jumps-8). While the wider market remains focused on macroeconomic trends and high valuations, individual company failures, such as the Datavault AI incident, create localized volatility and anxiety.

Furthermore, the event underscores the industry-wide challenges of institutionalizing digital assets. As noted by [CoinTelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/robinhoods-tenev-warns-us-tokenization-uncertainty-deters-serious-assets?utm_source=rss_feed&utm_medium=rss&utm_campaign=rss_partner_inbound), Robinhood CEO Vlad Tenev has recently emphasized that regulatory and structural uncertainty within the U.S. financial ecosystem continues to hinder the transition of traditional assets into digital frameworks. While Tenev’s comments were primarily directed at the challenges of tokenized equities, the Datavault AI experience suggests that operational custody remains a significant hurdle. Whether the barrier is regulatory or purely technical, as in the case of the Scilex deal, investors remain sensitive to any friction that prevents the seamless movement of capital in a digital-first environment.

## Future Outlook for Stakeholders

For Datavault AI, the immediate path forward requires resolving the withdrawal blockage and providing shareholders with a clear explanation of the deal's status. The market’s severe reaction suggests that investors are not currently willing to bear the risks of "trapped" capital in corporate treasuries. 

As the company attempts to recover, the incident will likely serve as a cautionary note for other firms exploring crypto-settled equity agreements. Institutional partners and regulators are expected to monitor the situation closely to determine if the $150 million agreement will be successfully executed or if it will be abandoned. For now, the market remains in a state of high uncertainty as it awaits further official disclosures from the involved parties.
