# VanEck Removes Gemini as Custodian for $1 Billion Digital Asset ETF Portfolio

On November 25, 2026, investment management firm VanEck announced a structural change to its suite of cryptocurrency-linked exchange-traded funds (ETFs). According to regulatory filings, the firm is removing Gemini as a clearing participant for three of its digital asset funds. These funds, which collectively hold nearly $1 billion in assets, include exposure to Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Solana. The move marks a shift in the operational architecture of these products, with the firm transitioning to a model that relies exclusively on Coinbase Custody for the safekeeping and clearing of its underlying crypto assets.

The termination filings, made public on November 25, 2026, indicate that the affected ETFs will no longer utilize Gemini Clearing, a service previously used to facilitate the settlement and custody of digital assets. While the firm has confirmed that Coinbase Custody will remain the primary custodian for all three funds, the filings do not name a replacement for Gemini. This consolidation suggests a streamlining of the administrative infrastructure supporting these assets. For investors, this transition represents a move toward a single-provider custodial framework for these instruments.

## Understanding the Role of Custodians in Crypto ETFs

The custody of digital assets is a fundamental component of spot cryptocurrency ETFs. Unlike traditional equity markets, where shares are held by central securities depositories, crypto assets require specialized infrastructure to manage private keys and ensure the secure transfer of tokens. Custodians act as the central point for asset security, responsible for holding tokens in cold storage or secure environments to prevent unauthorized access. By utilizing services like Coinbase Custody, asset managers aim to provide a regulated bridge between cryptocurrency markets and traditional financial systems.

The removal of Gemini from these specific funds highlights the ongoing evolution of service partner selection within the digital asset sector. Throughout 2026, the industry has placed an increased emphasis on regulatory compliance and operational stability. When a manager like VanEck modifies a clearing provider, it often prompts broader industry observation regarding vendor risk management policies. While the current filings do not explicitly detail the reasoning behind the removal of Gemini, the shift toward a single-provider model is a common strategy to simplify the complex accounting, reconciliation, and audit processes that occur when multiple custodians are involved in a fund’s lifecycle.

## Implications for Fund Governance and Risk Management

For investors, the primary concern when an ETF changes service providers is the impact on security and operational risk. By consolidating custody under Coinbase Custody for the Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Solana funds, VanEck centralizes the assets within a single institution's security architecture. While this reduces the complexity associated with moving assets between clearing houses during settlement, it also means the funds become entirely dependent on the systems and security protocols of that specific custodian. These changes in service providers are legally required disclosures intended to keep stakeholders informed about how their capital is being held.

This development occurs as the broader cryptocurrency market navigates macroeconomic uncertainty. As of November 25, 2026, institutional players are maintaining high levels of vigilance regarding their infrastructure. The choice of custodian is frequently viewed as a signal of institutional maturity, with large asset managers prioritizing firms that offer robust audit trails and proven track records in handling digital asset storage. By standardizing the clearing process, VanEck is likely attempting to create a more predictable experience for its fund operations, potentially reducing the likelihood of reconciliation discrepancies. This is not financial advice, and investors should review the official filings of any investment product to understand the specific custodial arrangements and risks involved.
