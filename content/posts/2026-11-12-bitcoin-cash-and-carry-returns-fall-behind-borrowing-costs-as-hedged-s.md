# Bitcoin Cash-and-Carry Returns Fall Behind Borrowing Costs as Hedged Strategy Squeezes Yields

Institutional and retail traders utilizing delta-neutral strategies in the cryptocurrency markets are facing diminished returns as financing costs outpace payouts. On November 10, 2026, pricing for hedged Bitcoin trades demonstrated that executing a classic cash-and-carry strategy yielded returns lower than the cost required to fund the underlying position, according to a report by [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/bitcoin-hedged-trade-borrowing-costs/).

The cash-and-carry approach allows market participants to gain exposure to Bitcoin while hedging against underlying price downturns. However, recent market dynamics have made this market-neutral posture increasingly difficult to execute profitably without relying on an outright upward spot price rally.

## Understanding the Bitcoin Cash-and-Carry Mechanics

The cash-and-carry trade is a well-established market arbitrage technique. In cryptocurrency markets, an investor executes the trade by purchasing spot Bitcoin while simultaneously selling (shorting) a derivative contract, such as a futures or perpetual contract, at a premium. By pairing a spot Bitcoin purchase with an equivalent short derivatives position, the trader neutralizes direct exposure to spot price volatility.

The profit from a cash-and-carry trade is derived from the convergence of the futures price and the spot price as the contract approaches its expiration date, or through collecting funding rates in perpetual futures markets. Because the short position protects the holder against a market decline, the strategy is designed to generate yield regardless of whether the broader market trends upward or downward.

However, the strategy relies heavily on favorable spread differentials and manageable borrowing costs to remain economically viable for traders financing their purchases using leverage.

## November 10 Pricing Reveals Negative Returns After Leverage Costs

Data from November 10, 2026, highlighted how narrow yield margins have become for hedged market participants. Based on market pricing on that date, a hedged Bitcoin cash-and-carry position offered a return of approximately $119 per Bitcoin (BTC) before accounting for associated transaction and operational costs, as reported by [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/bitcoin-hedged-trade-borrowing-costs/).

In contrast, an illustrative borrowing calculation assessing the capital costs needed to finance that spot purchase over a 17-day holding period required roughly $135 per BTC. Because the $135 financing expense exceeded the gross yield of $119 per BTC, the net return on the hedged structure turned negative before factoring in additional operational or trading fees.

This negative yield spread indicates that market participants who rely on borrowed capital or margin to enter cash-and-carry positions cannot currently cover their interest expenses using only the derivative premium yield.

## Impact of High Borrowing Costs on Delta-Neutral Arbitrage

When capital costs eclipse derivative premiums, the primary mechanism driving the cash-and-carry trade breaks down. In typical market conditions, robust bullish sentiment creates a higher premium on futures relative to spot prices, enabling arbitrageurs to lock in predictable yields that comfortably clear hurdle rates and borrowing fees.

When borrowing costs remain elevated relative to derivative yields, several broader market consequences emerge across the trading ecosystem:

* **Reduced Institutional Participation:** Yield-focused institutional desks and quantitative funds scale back cash-and-carry operations when net spreads compress below risk-free benchmarks or leverage rates.
* **Shift Toward Unhedged Positions:** Traders looking to maintain exposure to Bitcoin may be forced to forgo short hedges to capture returns, increasing their susceptibility to sudden spot market sell-offs.
* **Lower Futures Premiums:** Reduced arbitrage activity can impact liquidity across crypto derivatives exchanges, influencing the basis spread between spot and futures markets.

Without a wider spread between spot and futures prices or a decline in interest rates for financing spot crypto assets, hedged cash-and-carry strategies remain constrained.

## Strategic Considerations for Crypto Investors

For market participants relying on hedged strategies to generate steady returns, the current pricing environment underscores the necessity of analyzing total cost structures. Profitability in delta-neutral trades depends heavily on the source and cost of leverage, the length of the holding period, and collateral management.

Traders using owned capital without associated borrowing expenses may still capture the base return, although the gross yield of approximately $119 per BTC remains modest relative to historical cash-and-carry margins. For leveraged traders, executing the strategy under existing rates effectively requires betting on a directional spot rally to generate positive returns, undermining the market-neutral objective of the trade.
