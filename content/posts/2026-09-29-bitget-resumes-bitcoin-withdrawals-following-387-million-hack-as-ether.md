Cryptocurrency exchange Bitget has officially reopened Bitcoin (BTC) withdrawals after suffering a massive $387 million security breach, according to reportage from [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/bitget-says-bitcoin-withdrawals-open-after-hack/). While Bitcoin transactions are once again operational for users, the trading platform confirmed that withdrawals for Ethereum (ETH) and Tether (USDT) remain temporarily suspended.

The partial restoration of services marks a critical step in the exchange's emergency response plan following the multimillion-dollar incident. Public market data from September 28 indicated active Bitcoin and Ethereum futures trading taking place ahead of the scheduled 08:00 UTC restart for Bitcoin withdrawals. However, platform users holding Ethereum and Tether assets must continue to wait as the exchange works through its recovery protocols and security verification measures.

## Phased Operations and Futures Market Pre-Activity

The reopening of Bitcoin services followed careful monitoring of platform metrics and public order fills. On September 28, public trade fills revealed noticeable activity across BTC and ETH futures contracts prior to the designated 08:00 UTC operational milestone, as detailed by [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/bitget-says-bitcoin-withdrawals-open-after-hack/). This pre-withdrawal market engagement highlighted trader positioning across derivative products ahead of spot liquidity movements.

Despite the successful restart of Bitcoin processing, the decision to maintain restrictions on Ethereum and USDT underscores the complex, multi-asset nature of the hack investigation. Centralized exchanges often implement phased withdrawal schedules after major security incidents to prevent systemic liquidity runs, verify wallet balances, and isolate compromised smart contract logic or compromised infrastructure specific to individual token standards. ## Broader Regulatory and Stablecoin Scrutiny

The ongoing suspension of Tether (USDT) withdrawals on Bitget coincides with heightened regulatory focus surrounding the world's largest stablecoin. In a separate government development, a U.S. Senate report revealed that Tether USDT has been utilized to aid funding channels in Iran, as reported by [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/economy-news/tether-usdt-aids-iran-funding-senate-report-says-4920946). The findings highlight growing legislative concern over the use of pegged digital assets in international capital flows and illicit finance.

These concurrent developments place increased pressure on digital asset service providers to maintain transparent reserve management and rigorous compliance frameworks. As stablecoins like USDT serve as primary liquidity pairings across centralized exchanges including Bitget, heightened government oversight and asset-specific withdrawal delays present operational challenges for global traders managing cross-border exposure.
