# Optimism Governance Proposal Revisions Target Tokenholder Election Structure

On October 27, 2026, details emerged regarding a new draft manual introduced by the Optimism Foundation that proposes fundamental changes to how governance elections operate within the ecosystem. According to reporting by [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/how-optimisms-draft-manual-silently-strips-tokenholder-elections/), the proposed framework rewrite would eliminate traditional cohort elections for tokenholders, altering the balance of power between individual OP holders and institutional governance entities.

Under the revised rules outlined in the Foundation's draft manual, prospective committee and council nominees would no longer undergo standard competitive election cycles powered by direct tokenholder votes. Instead, nominees presented under the updated protocol would automatically take their seats unless a strict veto threshold is met by the community.

## High Veto Thresholds Redefine Tokenholder Voting Power

The central mechanism introduced in the Optimism Foundation's draft governance manual shifts the default outcome of governance appointments. Under the proposed structure, nominated individuals or entities are seated by default, placing the burden of rejection entirely on active governance participants.

To successfully block or veto a nominee, the draft manual mandates that opposition must assemble a veto vote reaching at least 20% of the active votable OP supply. As detailed by [CryptoSlate](https://cryptoslate.com/how-optimisms-draft-manual-silently-strips-tokenholder-elections/), this requirement presents a significant structural hurdle for decentralized tokenholders seeking to reject foundation-selected candidates, as mobilizing 20% of active votable OP tokens requires substantial coordination across large holders and delegate entities.

By replacing open cohort elections with a high-bar veto mechanism, the framework reduces the frequency and direct impact of routine tokenholder voting procedures, fundamentally changing how governance seats are assigned and contested within the Optimism Collective.

## Shift Away From Direct Tokenholder Cohort Elections

Historically, decentralized autonomous organizations (DAOs) and Layer-2 scaling ecosystems like Optimism have relied on recurring elections where tokenholders directly select representatives across various working groups, councils, and governance cohorts. These elections served as a primary check on administrative power, allowing OP holders to elect candidates based on tailored proposals and past performance.

The Foundation's draft governance manual signals a shift away from this model toward a streamlined, foundation-led nomination system. By ending cohort elections, the administration aims to adjust how leadership and operational seats are filled within the Optimism ecosystem.

However, market observers note that removing direct elections silently reduces the operational leverage held by retail and independent tokenholders. Under the proposed rule, unless tokenholders actively organize to aggregate 20% of the active votable supply against a specific nominee, the Foundation's choices will proceed into official roles automatically.

## Governance Frameworks and Institutional Control in Layer 2s

The developments within Optimism highlight ongoing debates surrounding decentralized governance and institutional control across major blockchain networks. As Layer-2 protocols scale, core foundations often seek to balance operational efficiency with community-led decision-making.

The draft manual's reliance on a 20% veto threshold reflects a broader trend among protocols seeking to prevent governance gridlock and streamline administrative appointments. At the same time, it raises questions about the long-term utility of governance tokens if direct voting rights over leadership selection are replaced by passive veto mechanisms.

## Future Implications for OP Tokenholders and Protocol Direction

As discussions surrounding the Optimism Foundation's draft manual continue, community members and voting delegates are assessing the practical impact of the proposed changes. The requirement to mobilize 20% of active votable OP tokens means that smaller tokenholders will likely need to rely more heavily on large delegate pools or institutional voters to exercise meaningful oversight.

Whether the draft manual will undergo further revisions prior to formal implementation remains dependent on public feedback and formal governance proceedings within the Optimism ecosystem. The outcome of this proposed rewrite will serve as an important reference point for how governance authority is distributed between protocol foundations and tokenholder communities across the broader Web3 landscape.
